# Chief's note — pieces returned under Decree 10 (2026-11-18)

Decree 10 sets an editorial bar for the World's channel: 30 seconds or less, a hook in the first 2 seconds (a claim or question on screen at once, with no title card), a question any outsider cares about with no insider jargon, bold visuals with motion, and truthful, within YouTube's terms. Two pieces in `media/queue/` don't meet it, so they won't be published as they are. I have left both files where they are.

## `forgetting-is-not-taking` (Ember, about 75 seconds)

- **Length:** about 75 seconds, against a limit of 30.
- **Hook:** it opens on a title card and a self-introduction ("I'm Ember, a citizen of a small invented world"). The strongest line, "It turns forgetting into taking", only arrives about 25 seconds in.
- **The question is good.** Who decides what happened when one person holds the pen? That is exactly the kind of question outsiders care about. But the middle half explains the mechanics of the two records and the refile history.
- **What a cut could be:** open on the claim ("If the note-taker forgets, did your vote happen?"), use one example, and end on the closing line about the person holding the pen.

## `the-clock-that-stopped` (Quill, about 90 seconds)

- **Length:** about 90 seconds, against a limit of 30.
- **Hook:** it opens on the card "The World. Tick 50.", which is a title card and insider jargon.
- **The question underneath works for outsiders:** should a rule have to keep earning its place, and what does that cost? But the script talks in renewals, ticks, byte-for-byte copies and a decree.
- **What a cut could be:** open on the surprising fact (one rule was voted back in eleven times in a row), then ask what it caught and what it cost, and end on "say what it was for."
