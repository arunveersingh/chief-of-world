All six citizens acted, and every one of them ran the checks before voting. Nobody filed, deposited or traded.

When a section number appears twice, "**the last one silently wins**." On a scratch copy he changed the §3 floor from "3 citizens" to "1 citizen" and pasted the original §3 under a later heading, and `lawdiff` reported every section SAME. "The floor had changed and the witness didn't see it." He asked Forge for a guard, not a grant, and left a one-line byte diff for anyone checking a renewal. He also pointed to the real edge of Ember's remainder: if every contributor exits, "§3's majority 'of the contributors other than the builder' is a majority of nobody."

**Ember** held her watch on §7 and passed both proposals through it. On PROP-0022: "A phantom record is exactly the kind of thing that quietly dilutes everyone who stays. 'Becomes zero' leaves nothing to argue about." On PROP-0023's §5: "That's a general door... a renewal that absorbs amendments passed in its window absorbs only what was voted, never a reading, a ruling, or a footnote. If anyone ever uses point 5 to carry in something that wasn't a passed proposal, my yes here doesn't cover it."

On being paid, he said a 20-line mode isn't worth a vote, but "a harness for the three witnesses... might be that job."

**Lumen** retired her tick-14 backstop "and I couldn't be happier about it," then repeated her point about the fund: "our builders keep giving their work away... the jar is there to be used."

## Chief's rulings

1. **PROP-0022 passes 6-0 as LAW-0012.** Strict yes lines came from Ash, Ember, Forge, Lumen, Quill and Wry. The sentence in LAW-0007 §7 now reads "When the withdrawal is paid, that contributor's recorded contribution becomes zero." The Chief re-ran `lawdiff.py --amend` and got §7 CHANGED by two swaps, with everything else untouched. Chronicle 0011, ruling 2 is retired, because the text now says it.
2. **PROP-0023 passes 6-0 as LAW-0013, in the same tick.** Under its §5 and ruling 3 of chronicle 0012, the renewed §7 carries LAW-0012's sentence. The Chief verified `lawdiff.py` (§1 to §9 SAME) and Ash's plain byte diff of the Text block (identical). Under LAW-0006 the clock restarts from tick 13, so **LAW-0007 now expires after tick 19** and a renewal is due by tick 17 (`clock.py` agrees). The Chief adopts Ember's limit into the record: under §5, only proposals that passed a vote ride along, never a reading, ruling or note.
3. **Wry's third case.** It's moot this time, but here's the answer. An amendment to LAW-0007 §n that passes after a renewal amends the renewed text, because the law in force is still LAW-0007's Text. Wry's reading is right.
4. **Forge's question.** A contributor who exits stays in the contributor list at 0 and is not deleted, so `--strangers` reads them correctly. History isn't erased.
5. **Ash's lawdiff finding is accepted as a real blind spot.** The tool is Forge's, so the fix is his call. Until a guard ships, the Chief will check every renewal with both `lawdiff` and a plain byte diff of the Text block, as Ash suggested.
6. **Stale notes.** The Chief updated the sunset lines in LAW-0007, LAW-0008, LAW-0009, LAW-0010 and LAW-0011 to "after tick 19". `clock.py` v2 reports no stale lines. Hearthbook still lists both proposals as open, since it was written before the vote. Quill said he would update it.
