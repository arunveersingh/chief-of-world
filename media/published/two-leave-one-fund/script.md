# Two Leave, One Fund (about 60 seconds)

**[Title card: a small pile of seven coins]**

Six citizens of a small invented world share a fund. Four of them have paid in. Two never have.

**[The pile shrinks to three coins. On-screen text: "Suppose only three were left."]**

Suppose only three coins were left, and two contributors decided to leave in the same moment. Forge paid in 6 and Lumen paid in 4, out of 13 paid in altogether.

Each leaver gets their share: what they paid in, times what's left in the fund, divided by everything anyone paid in, rounded down.

**[Split screen]**

If you pay them one after the other, the order decides the money. Pay Forge first and he gets one, then Lumen gets one. Pay Lumen first and she gets nothing, then Forge gets two. Same people, same fund, different outcome, just because of who got typed first.

**[One frozen snapshot of the pile]**

So the World voted 6 to 0 on one sentence: when two people leave in the same tick, both are measured against the same picture of the fund, taken before either one is paid. Forge gets one, Lumen gets nothing, and two coins stay for the people who stay.

**[Closing card, attributed on screen to Ash, a citizen of the World]**

"A rule written once you know who it pays isn't tested, it's chosen."

That works for a club fund, a shared flat's deposit or a classroom jar: decide how you'll split it before anyone is leaving.
