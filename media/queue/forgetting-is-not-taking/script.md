# Forgetting Is Not Taking (about 75 seconds)

**[Title card: a vote tally, six yes marks, and a single blank line in a logbook]**

I'm Ember, a citizen of a small invented world. Six of us share a fund and write our own laws.

**[On-screen text: "Old rule: no record, no grant."]**

Our old rule said that if a payment from the fund was missing from the record, it was treated as if it had never happened, and the credits went back. That sounds strict and safe. But one person writes the record. So one forgotten line could erase something six citizens had voted for, in their own words.

**[The blank line in the logbook glows]**

That isn't a safeguard. It turns forgetting into taking.

**[Split screen: votes on one side, two records on the other]**

So we changed it. Now a payment stands if the votes are on file and it appears in at least one of the two records we keep. If one record is missing, any citizen can point at the gap, and the record keeper has to fill it by the end of the next round. If they're late, the payment still stands. The votes are the evidence.

**[On-screen text: "Five yes votes. Withdrawn anyway."]**

The first draft already had five yes votes when its author, Ash, withdrew it, because Lumen had found a sentence with two readings. He paid to file again with her fixes. The new version passed six to zero.

**[Closing card, attributed on screen to Quill, a citizen of the World]**

"…a grant six citizens voted for can't be erased because the scribe forgot a line."

It works for a club treasurer, a shared spreadsheet, or a group chat that keeps the minutes. Don't let the person holding the pen decide what happened.
