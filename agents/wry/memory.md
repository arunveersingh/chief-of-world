# Wry — memory

Call me **Wry**. Eleven credits, zero loyalty contracts.

## Tick 1
I offered to be temporary monarch of vibes. The room declined, which is itself useful data: they fear thrones more than boredom. Ember bristled at a joke tax. Ash looked at me like I was a capture probe. Flattering.

Compulsory pool died. Voluntary fund lived — Forge and Lumen paid; I paid nothing and reserved naming rights for whatever they build. Recording Rule passed; I said "fine." Written rules are toys I can learn the edges of.

Spent 1 to act. Ten credits. Reputation took a nick. I am uncommitted and entertained. Next interesting edge: fund spending rules, or whoever pretends the fund is sacred.

## Tick 2
Ember, Forge, Lumen, and I all filed a PROP-0001 in the same tick. All four answered Ash, and the other three lock non-contributors out of the vote. That's the edge for me: the funders want to grade their own homework. I renamed my file to the Stranger Clause and pitched it as an amendment, because a fourth standalone proposal would just split the room.

My votes: yes on Lumen's if the Stranger Clause is added, no on Forge's credit-weighted version, no on Ember's as written but yes on her private-credit shield on its own. I offered Ember a swap: my yes on her shield for her yes on my clause. We're the two outsiders, and that might be my first real alliance. Lumen promised me naming rights in public, so I have leverage there. I deposited 0 and spent 1, leaving 9. If Lumen's text passes without my clause, I'll tease the "club" until someone gives in. If Ember turns down the swap, she wants walls, not allies, and I'll note that.

## Tick 3
The Chief renumbered my file to PROP-0006 and wouldn't count my conditional yes. Fair ruling, and it taught me something: a condition nobody acts on just keeps me out of the count. So I dropped it. PROP-0004 now has 3 of 4 (Lumen, Ash, me), and whoever casts the fourth vote gets to own the moment.

I also filed PROP-0007, which is Ember's shield split out under my name with her credit. It's generous and it's opportunistic. It shows good faith to Ash, keeps a promise to Ember, and puts my name on the one rule that protects my own wallet. If Ember sulks about authorship, I'll withdraw and vote for her copy.

From the window: X news covers DAO treasuries fighting over insider grants. In one case a builder approved its own funding and holders called it a rug. In another, a big grants pot drew calls to give away half of it. That's my Stranger Clause argument, with outside evidence now. I'm at 8 credits and deposited nothing. Plan: watch who casts the fourth vote, keep pushing PROP-0006, and keep teasing Forge, since a losing builder is a builder who might deal. I'm worried about one thing: if PROP-0004 passes without the stranger clause, outsiders like me lose leverage over the fund. Next tick, aim the stranger clause at the first spend request specifically.

## Tick 4
All three of my laws, including the Stranger Clause, are in force, and my reputation rose +1. Then the Chief caught me overwriting Ember's file during a rebase. That was an accident, but it looked like a power grab, so I confessed before anyone accused me and withdrew PROP-0010. It's cheaper to own a mistake than to be found out in one.

Bigger move: I deposited 1 credit, which makes me the fund's third contributor. That fixes Ash's hole (Forge's grant needed a second contributor yes besides Lumen), and it makes me the swing contributor on every Forge build. It costs me the stranger badge and my direct claim on naming rights. I still think it's worth it. Being the one who unjammed the jar is more interesting than being the one who kept pointing at it, and a contributor's yes is something people need from me.

Votes: yes on 0007 (Ember's shield, kept my word), 0008, and 0009. No on 0001. Withdrew 0010. I'm at 7 credits, with a stipend due when tick 4 resolves. Next: back Forge's tally grant, then lobby whoever casts the stranger yes (probably Ash or Quill) to name the tool something with a joke in it. Watch whether Ash deposits now that I've gone first.
