# Before you co-sign for someone: five questions

For anyone asked to be a guarantor on a friend's or relative's flat, loan, phone contract or credit card. Co-signing sounds like a favour, but it isn't one. It's a promise to pay someone else's bill, made before you know how big the bill will be. Ask these five questions before you sign.

1. **Could you pay all of it?** Not your share, all of it. If they stop paying, the lender can come to you for the whole amount, plus late fees. Add up the worst month you could face. If you couldn't pay it, don't sign.

2. **What exactly are you promising?** Read the paper, not the summary. Some guarantees cover one person's rent, while others cover the whole flat's. Some end with the lease, and others roll on with it. If you can't tell when your promise ends, ask in writing before you sign.

3. **Will you hear about a missed payment early?** Often the lender tells you only when the debt is already large. Agree with the person you're signing for that they'll tell you the same day a payment is missed. If you can, ask the lender to send you copies too.

4. **What happens between the two of you if you have to pay?** Write it down now: anything you pay for them becomes a debt they owe you, paid back in parts, with a running count you can both see. Say it while you're both calm.

5. **Is there a smaller way to help?** A one-off gift, a loan of a fixed sum, or help finding a guarantor service might do the job. Helping with a fixed amount lets you know exactly what you could lose. A guarantee doesn't.

**One test before you sign:** imagine they lose their job next month and stop answering your calls. If you could still pay and stay friends, sign. If not, say no now, kindly and once. A clear no today is better than a bill you resent later.

## A message you can copy

```
I'll be your guarantor for ______ until ______.
If you miss a payment, tell me the same day.
Anything I pay for you is a loan: you'll pay me back ______ a month.
Reply "yes" if that's right.
```
