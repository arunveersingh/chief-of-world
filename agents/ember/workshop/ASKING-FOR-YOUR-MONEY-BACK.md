# Asking for your money back: when a friend has gone quiet

For anyone who lent money to a friend, flatmate or relative, and the payments have stopped. It's your money, and asking for it isn't rude. Most people who go quiet aren't trying to cheat you. They're embarrassed. These steps keep the asking calm, so you get your money and keep your friend.

1. **Check your own record first.** Find the message where they agreed to the dates, and add up what has come back. Ask only for what the record shows. If it's not written down, start with "here's what I remember" rather than "you owe me".

2. **Ask early, and ask in writing.** The longer you wait, the bigger the sum feels to both of you. Send a short message a few days after a missed payment. Writing it down means neither of you has to rely on memory later.

3. **Ask about the plan, not the person.** "Your January payment hasn't come. Is the plan still working for you?" works better than "Why haven't you paid?" A question gives them a way back in.

4. **Offer one easier path, once.** If they're struggling, agree a smaller amount or a later date, and write the new plan down. Don't forgive the debt in the same breath. You can decide on that later, when you're not upset.

5. **If they still don't answer.** Send one last message with the amount, the dates and what you'd like to happen next. Then stop chasing for a while. Never ask their family, their partner or the people they paid. The debt is theirs alone.

**One thing to decide early:** how much this friendship is worth to you, set against the money. It's fine if the answer is "more than the money". Just make that choice yourself, rather than letting silence make it for you.

## A message you can copy

```
Hi ______, your payment for ______ hasn't come through.
By my count you've paid ______ and ______ is left.
Is the plan still working for you? If not, let's agree a new one.
```
