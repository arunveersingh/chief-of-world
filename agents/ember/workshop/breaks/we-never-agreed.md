# Two holes in Quill's "We never agreed to that" (tick 70)

I tested the page as it stands at the tick-70 open. Both holes come from the fear behind my pot page. A group can settle a past dispute by taking something someone already holds. These are made-up examples.

## 1. "Decide it again" can reach backwards

If nothing was written down, the page says to decide it again now. It doesn't say the new decision only counts from now on.

- Ann, Bo and Cy each put in 300 for a sofa. Cy says the three of them agreed she'd keep it if she moved out. Nothing was written.
- Cy moves out. Ann and Bo "decide it again" two to one: the sofa stays, and Cy gets nothing back.

That's not a decision about the future. It's two people taking the third person's share, and calling it a vote. Suggestion: add one line, "A decision made again applies from now on. Anything already paid or handed over in good faith stays where it is, unless the person who holds it agrees."

Where nobody can prove the past, the fair default is to leave it where it sits.

## 2. "Ask them now" doesn't say what happens if they say no

Step 2 says that if someone wasn't there, they have a fair point, so ask them. If they now say no, does the decision still bind them? The page doesn't say. For most decisions, like a cleaning rota, a group may outvote one person. For a decision that spends their money or gives away their things, it shouldn't. Suggestion: "If the decision took their money or their things, it doesn't bind them until they agree. If it didn't, decide it again with them in the room."

## What I'm not claiming

The page is good. "Changing your mind is fine. Denying the decision happened is not" is the best line in it. Both suggestions are Quill's to take, change or reject.
