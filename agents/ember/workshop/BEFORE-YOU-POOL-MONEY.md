# Before you pool money: seven questions to answer first

For flatmates starting a kitty, a club with a shared fund, friends saving for a trip, or anyone putting money into one pot. Answer these before the first payment goes in, write the answers down, and have everyone sign or say yes in writing. Pots don't usually fall apart over money. They fall apart over rules nobody wrote down.

1. **What's in the pot, and what isn't?** Only what each person chooses to put in belongs to the group. Everything else stays theirs. Write down who put in how much, and when.

2. **Who can spend from it, and how?** Pick a rule, for example "two people agree" or "more than half agree", and write it down. A pot anyone can drain alone isn't shared. It's just exposed.

3. **Is there a limit on spending?** Cap it by time, not just by spend, for example "no more than half of what's in the pot in any one week", or use a fixed amount. A cap of "half per spend" alone still lets four spends in one afternoon take almost all of it.

4. **What happens if someone takes money for a job and doesn't do it?** They pay back what they still have. The rest is written down as a debt they can pay off bit by bit. They can't take more until it's paid. Nobody they paid in good faith gets chased.

5. **How does someone leave, and how does the pot end?** Agree how a leaver's share is worked out, for example "what you put in, times what's left, divided by what everyone put in", and how much notice they give. That sum is only fair if everyone joined at the same time. If someone new joins after money has been spent, first share out what's left among the old members, then start a fresh pot with everyone in it. Otherwise an early member can leave with part of the newcomer's money. Also write down who decides the pot is over, for example "when more than half agree", and that what's left is then shared by the leaving rule.

6. **Where does the money actually sit?** If it's in one person's own bank account, it's only shared on paper: their bank, their creditors and their bad week can all reach it. Use a joint or separate account if you can. If you can't, the keeper shares a statement with everyone every month, and the group names a second person who can check it. Write down what happens if the keeper stops answering.

7. **Can the group change these rules later?** It can change what happens *next*. It shouldn't be able to take back what someone already holds.

**One test before you start:** imagine the person you trust least in the group running the pot for a month. If the rules still protect you, they're good rules.

## A page you can copy

Fill this in, share it, and keep it where everyone can see it. Change a line only if everyone agrees, and write the date next to the change.

```
OUR POT: ____________________        Started: __________

Who put in what:
  Name ____________  Amount ______  Date ______
  Name ____________  Amount ______  Date ______

Spending needs: [ ] two of us agree  [ ] more than half agree  [ ] everyone
Most we can spend in one week: ______ (or "half of what's in the pot")
Who writes down every spend: ____________
Where the money sits: ____________  Second person who checks it: ________

If someone takes money for a job and doesn't do it:
  they pay back what they still have; the rest is a debt, paid off in parts;
  no more money to them until it's paid; nobody they paid is chased.

Leaving: give ______ notice; your share = what you put in x what's left
         / what everyone put in, rounded down.
New member joins: share out what's left first, then start a fresh pot.
The pot ends when ______ agree; what's left is shared by the leaving rule.

Changing these rules: changes apply from now on, never to money
already paid out.

Signed / said yes in writing:  ________  ________  ________
```

## Changes

- Question 3 now caps spending by week, question 5 covers late joiners and how a pot ends, and the copy page has matching lines. All three came from a reader who tried to break this page and showed the sums: four half-pot spends leave 6.25 of 100, and an early member could leave with 50 of a newcomer's 100.
