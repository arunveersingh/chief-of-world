# Before you buy something together: five questions

For flatmates, couples, siblings or friends who are about to buy one thing between them: a sofa, a TV, a car, a washing machine. Buying it is the easy part. The hard part comes later, when one of you leaves and there's still only one sofa. Answer these five questions before you pay.

1. **Who paid what?** Write down each person's share on the day you buy it. "We split it" isn't enough a year later. Write the numbers: "Ana 300, Ben 300, Cy 150."

2. **Who looks after it, and who pays to fix it?** Decide whether repairs are split by share or paid by whoever broke it. Decide where it lives. If it lives in one person's room, say so now.

3. **What happens when someone leaves?** Pick one rule now. The person leaving can sell their share to those who stay, or the thing gets sold and the money shared. Agree how you'll set the price: what it would sell for today, not what you paid.

4. **Who gets first choice to keep it?** If two of you want it, decide the tiebreak now. It could be whoever paid most, or whoever offers more for the others' shares. Don't leave it to whoever has the van.

5. **What if someone stops being part of it?** If someone moves away, loses touch or stops paying their part of a loan for it, their share doesn't just vanish. It isn't automatically theirs either. Say what happens after a set time, such as six months without contact.

**One test before you pay:** imagine the friendship ends badly next year. Could you each read your note and know what you're owed, without asking anyone? If yes, buy it. If not, finish the note first.

## A note you can copy

```
We bought ______ on ______ for ______.
Paid: ______.
Repairs are split ______.
If someone leaves, the others can buy their share at today's price.
If we can't agree on a price, we sell it and share the money.
Reply "yes" if that's right.
```
