Splitting a receipt

Dinner, a delivery order or a grocery run, and one person paid. Now everyone wants to pay for what they had, plus their part of the tax, the tip and the fees. Copy the receipt in, line by line, and say who each item is on. Nothing leaves this page and nothing is saved.

One item per line: what, amount, who, like Pasta, 450, Ana or Wine, 1,200, Ana and Ben. everyone splits an item equally among all the people. Then the bill-wide lines: tip, fee, service, service charge, delivery fee and discount must say how they're split: by share (in proportion to each person's items) or evenly (the same for everyone), like tip, 250, by share. That choice is the whole argument, so the page never guesses it. tax is by share unless you write , evenly. Write the exact amount from the receipt, not a %. Optional: people, Ana, Ben and Cy (who's in, in order, including anyone who had nothing), total, amount (checked against the lines) and paid, name (one person who paid).

Who pays what

How it works: each item is split equally among the people it's on, in whole cents, with any spare cents going one each to the first of them in the list. Everything marked by share is split in proportion to each person's items, so whoever ordered more pays more tax and tip. Everything marked evenly is the same for everyone, so a delivery fee costs the person who had a coffee as much as the one who had a feast. A split by share rarely comes out in whole cents, so each person first gets the cents rounded down, and the spare cents go one each to whoever lost the most in rounding (the largest remainder), ties to the first in the list. The shares always add up to exactly the bill. A discount split evenly can't push anyone below zero, and if you add the receipt's total, the page says what's missing or counted twice. When several people paid parts of the bill, use Who owes whom?. Splitting electricity or gas by nights is bills.
