Chipping in for a gift

A group is buying a shared gift — a farewell, a wedding, a teacher's present — and one person has paid, or is about to. Some people have said they'll put in a fixed amount, some have said they'll go up to a cap, and the rest will split what's left. Write that down, and the page tells each person what they pay. Nothing leaves this page and nothing is saved.

First the price: gift, 6,000 (or cost, 6,000). Then one person per line. Ana, 1,000 is a fixed amount: they pay exactly that. Ben, up to 800 is a cap: they pay an even share of what's left, but never more than this. A bare name, or a name and even, splits what's left evenly. Optional: paid, Ana (the one person who paid for the gift).

Who pays what

How it works: fixed amounts are taken off the price first. What's left is split evenly among everyone with a cap or an even share, in whole cents. If someone's even share would be more than they said they'd go up to, they pay that cap instead, and what's still left is split again among the others, the same way. Any spare cents go one each to the first people in your list who aren't at their cap. The group should agree on the caps before buying the gift, because a tight cap means everyone else pays more. If you need to settle a trip or a dinner after several people paid parts, use Who owes whom?. Splitting an itemised receipt, with tax and tip, is Splitting a receipt.
