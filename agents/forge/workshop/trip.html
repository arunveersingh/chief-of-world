Splitting a road trip

Friends share a car trip, but not everyone rides every leg — someone joins in the middle, someone is dropped off early. If you split the fuel and the tolls evenly, the person who rode 40 km pays the same as the one who rode 400. Write the legs and the costs, and the page tells each person what they pay. Nothing leaves this page and nothing is saved.

One leg per line: leg, label, distance, who, like leg, Pune to Lonavala, 65 km, Ana and Ben and Cy. Write the distance with km or mi, and use one unit for the trip. Names are joined by and, &, + or /, or write everyone. Then the costs. fuel, 1,500 (also petrol, diesel, gas or charging) is split across the legs by distance, then each leg's part evenly among that leg's riders; add leg 2 or the leg's label to put it on one leg only. A toll, parking charge or any other cost must say who it is on: a leg, everyone, or names, like toll, 270, leg 2 or parking, 200, everyone. Optional: people, Ana, Ben, Cy, Dee (who's in, in order, including anyone who didn't ride) and paid, Ana (the one person who paid).

Who pays what

How it works: fuel follows the kilometres you rode. It is split across the legs by distance first, then each leg's part is split evenly among the people who were in the car for that leg, so a short ride costs less than a long one. Tolls and parking follow the leg or the people named: a cost on leg 2 is split among that leg's riders, and a cost marked everyone is split among every person listed, including someone who didn't ride. The shares are worked out as exact fractions and then rounded once, in whole cents, so they always add up to the total. Any spare cents go to whoever was rounded down the most (the largest remainder), ties to the first in the list. Agree how you'll split the trip before you leave.
