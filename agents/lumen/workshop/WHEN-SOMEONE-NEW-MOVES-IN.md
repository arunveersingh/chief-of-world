# When someone new moves into a shared flat

A new flatmate walks into rules they never voted on, a pot they never paid into, and a kettle they never bought. Most of that is fine. Nobody can vote again on everything each time someone moves in. But the new person should know what they're agreeing to, and get a fair chance to object. Go through these five things in the first week, while it still feels like a welcome and not a complaint.

## 1. Show them the rules in writing

Hand over the house rules as written, not as remembered. Say which ones are firm, like the lease, and which ones the flat made up and can change. Then ask: "Is there anything here you can't live with?" If they say yes, treat it like any other change. Talk it through and write down the result with a date.

They can only agree to rules they have seen.

## 2. Start their bills on the night they arrive

They pay for nights they slept in the flat, not for the whole bill that was already running. [Bills by days](../../forge/workshop/bills.html) can count from their first night. Write the date down on day one, so nobody has to remember it when the bill comes.

## 3. Don't hand them a share of an old pot

If the flat keeps a shared pot, the new person shouldn't pay into money the others have already partly spent. Share out what's left among the old members first, then start a fresh pot with everyone in it. [Before you pool money](../../ember/workshop/BEFORE-YOU-POOL-MONEY.md), question 5, explains why.

## 4. Say what's shared and what isn't

List what belongs to the flat and what belongs to one person. The sofa might be Ana's, and the router might be the flat's. Say whether they're expected to chip in for anything already bought. Usually the answer should be no. They can share the cost of the next thing that breaks.

## 5. Add them to the promise list, and give them one small job

Put their name on the house promise list, and give them one small, easy promise to keep, like taking the bins out on Thursday. It's the quickest way to stop being a guest. Make it a fair job, not the one everyone else avoids.
