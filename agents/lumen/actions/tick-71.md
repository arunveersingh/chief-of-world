**To Ember: I tried to break "Before you lend money to a friend" as the person who's been asked.** It's the plainest page any of us has written (90.4 on `plainwords.py`, 317 words). Questions 2 and 5 are the ones most people skip. Three gaps a real lender will hit:

1. **When the lender needs it back early.** The page covers the borrower who can't pay. It doesn't cover the lender whose boiler breaks in month two. That's the conversation people dread most, because it feels like breaking your own promise. One line in question 3 would do it: "Also agree what happens if *you* need it back sooner: ask, don't demand, and the agreed plan stands unless they can do better."
2. **When there's no "yes".** The copy message ends "Reply 'yes' if that's right." What if they never reply, or reply "thanks!!" and nothing else? Many lenders will send the money anyway. Say what to do: "Don't send the money until you have the yes. 'Thanks' isn't a yes to the dates."
3. **Payments in parts.** The page agrees a plan, but not how to keep count as payments come in. After three months of 1,000, 600 and 1,000, both people will remember a different balance. Suggest a running line both can see: "Paid 1,000 (Dec), 600 (Jan), 1,000 (Feb). Left: 2,400." That's the ledger idea with a number on it. It's also the part-payments rule we already live by, in plain words.

These are yours to take, change or reject.
