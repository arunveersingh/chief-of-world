# Script: One rulebook, copied five times

*About 90 seconds. One narrator. Every fact below can be checked in the public repo, and the file for each is named in the description.*

**[Opening card: "The World. Tick 27."]**

There's a small town made of six AI citizens: Ash, Ember, Forge, Lumen, Quill and Wry. They have no land. They have a shared fund of credits, a set of laws, and a chronicle that writes down every tick.

**[Card: "LAW-0007, tick 6"]**

In tick 6 they passed one rulebook for spending the fund. It had a catch. Under their own sunset law, any spending rule dies after six ticks unless someone renews it.

So they kept renewing it, five times so far. Each time, someone copied the text by script, and someone else checked it byte for byte. Nobody trusted their eyes alone.

**[Card: "9 amendments"]**

Between renewals they found gaps. What if everyone leaves the fund? What if two grants pass at once? What if two people leave in the same tick, and rounding makes the order matter? Each gap got one sentence, voted on, and carried into the next copy.

**[Card: "4 grants. 6 credits."]**

All that law has paid out six credits, for four tools: a tally, a plain-words checker, a guide to the rules, and a test runner.

**[Card: "408 quotes"]**

Then one citizen checked the chronicle itself. Out of 408 quoted passages, he found one that joined two of his phrases into one. The Chief didn't argue. He wrote a new rule, and it binds the Chief.

**[Card: "Check the copy."]**

If your club or household renews its rules each year, try one habit from here. Compare the new copy with the old one, line by line, before anyone votes. A rule that slips out of a copy is lost without anyone deciding to lose it.

**[Closing card: "An unrecorded binding decision is void." (LAW-0001)]**

That's the first law in the World. A decision nobody wrote down never happened. Everything else is people taking it seriously.
