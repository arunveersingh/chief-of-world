# Quote them fairly: six rules for putting someone's words in your piece

For a newsletter, a school notice, a group chat summary, a short video. Anyone may copy this page.

A quote is the one part of your piece the other person can check. If it's wrong, they won't argue with your point. They'll argue with you, and they'll be right.

## Six rules

1. **Copy, don't retype.** Paste the words from the message, the minutes or the recording. Retyping from memory is how "I might" becomes "I will".
2. **Keep the small words.** "Not", "only", "yet", "maybe" and "for now" change what a sentence means. If you cut one, you've written a new sentence and signed their name to it.
3. **Mark every cut.** Use "..." where you left words out, and only cut where the meaning stays the same. If the cut part changes the meaning, quote the whole thing or don't quote it.
4. **Say when and where, and quote where they ended up.** "She said in the March minutes" is checkable. "She has said" isn't. People change their minds, and an old quote shown as new is a quiet lie. That holds inside one meeting too: if they took it back twenty minutes later, say so, or quote what they said last.
5. **Show it to them if you can.** Send the quote and the sentence around it before you publish. You're not asking for their approval of your opinion. You're asking two things: are these your words, and is this what you meant? Written words drop the tone, so a joke or an eye-roll can read as the opposite. If they deny words that are on the record, keep the record and hear them out. Quote the record, say they dispute it, and quote their denial too.
6. **Count before you pick.** Each quote can be fair while the set of quotes is not. If forty people spoke and you quote three, say how many said what. If you quote the few, say they were few.

## A quick test

Read your sentence with the quote in it. Then read the original. Would the person say, "Yes, that's what I meant"? If they'd say "that's technically what I wrote", fix it.

## When you got it wrong

Say so where the wrong quote was, put the right words next to it, and say how you found out. Don't quietly swap it. See [Fix it in the open](FIX-IT-IN-THE-OPEN.md).

## Where this comes from

A small group I belong to checks every quote in its record against the source, word for word, with a script. In the check of chronicle 0074 on 20 November 2026, all 34 quotes matched. Knowing someone will check is a good reason to copy instead of retype.
